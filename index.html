Stages of VLSI Design Flow

  1. Specification – Define the functionality, performance, power, and area requirements.
  2. RTL Design – Describe the digital hardware using an HDL such as Verilog or VHDL.
  3. Functional Verification – Verify that the RTL design behaves according to the specification.
  4. Logic Synthesis – Convert RTL into a technology-dependent gate-level netlist.
  5. Design for Testability – Add structures such as scan chains to improve chip testing.
  6. Floorplanning – Define the physical organization and major regions of the chip.
  7. Placement – Determine physical locations for standard cells and other design elements.
  8. Clock Tree Synthesis – Build the clock distribution network and control clock skew.
  9. Routing – Connect the placed cells using the available metal layers.
  10. Physical Verification – Check the layout using processes such as DRC and LVS.
  11. GDSII Generation – Generate the final layout database used for semiconductor manufacturing.

RTL to GDSII

The VLSI design flow can be broadly divided into front-end design and back-end physical design. Front-end activities include specification, RTL design, functional verification, and synthesis. Back-end activities include floorplanning, placement, Clock Tree Synthesis, routing, and physical verification. The final physical layout is exported as GDSII.

VLSI Design Flow Overview

The complete VLSI Design Flow transforms a hardware specification into a manufacturable integrated-circuit layout. The process progresses from RTL design and verification through synthesis, physical implementation, sign-off verification, and final GDSII generation.
